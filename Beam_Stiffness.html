Overview

The stiffness method analyzes beams with the same steps used for trusses, with two changes. First, beam members resist bending, so the degrees of freedom at each node are a vertical displacement and a rotation. Second, beams often carry loads between their nodes, while the stiffness equations relate forces and displacements only at the nodes.

The principle of superposition handles the loads between nodes. The intended structure is written as the sum of two structures:

  • The loads off nodes structure treats every member as fixed at both ends and carries only the loads applied between nodes. The resulting fixed-end forces are tabulated and are taken as given here. They come from the force method, which we will discuss later.
  • The loads on nodes structure keeps the original supports and carries the reverse of every fixed-end force, plus any loads that were already applied at a node. This is the structure that the stiffness method solves.

Choose supports, properties, and loads for the two-member beam below, then follow the three steps down the page. Click on a member to see how its properties affect its stiffness matrix and its contribution to the global system of equations. At the end, the two structures are added back together to give the displacements, member forces, reactions, and diagrams of the intended structure.

Tip

Every load, end force, and displacement on this page uses one sign convention: forces and displacements are positive upward, and moments and rotations are positive counterclockwise. A downward load is therefore entered as a negative number. Axial deformation is neglected, so pins and rollers behave identically. The defaults correspond to a steel beam (E = 200 GPa) with I = 4×10⁻⁴ m⁴.

Loads Off Nodes Structure

Each member is fixed at both ends and carries only its own loads between nodes. Because every node is locked, no node moves in this structure. The forces that the fixed ends apply to a member are its fixed-end forces, listed below using the sign convention above.

Loads On Nodes Structure

The original supports are restored, and every load now acts at a node. Each node receives the reverse of the fixed-end forces from the members that meet there, plus any load that was applied at that node in the intended structure:

\[ F_i = P_i - \sum_{\text{members}} \text{FEF}_i . \]

Gray rows act directly on a restrained degree of freedom. The support takes these loads without the beam deforming, so they do not enter the known forces \(\mathbf{F}_k\) in Step 2.

Step 2: Apply the Stiffness Method to the Loads On Nodes Structure

From here, the steps match the truss stiffness method. Everything in this step describes the loads on nodes structure.

Element Stiffness Matrix

Each member has a vertical displacement and a rotation at each of its two ends. Both members are horizontal and axial deformation is neglected, so the local and global coordinate systems coincide and no transformation matrix is needed.

\[\begin{equation} \mathbf{k}_e = \dfrac{EI}{L^3} \begin{bmatrix} 12 & 6L & -12 & 6L \\ 6L & 4L^2 & -6L & 2L^2 \\ -12 & -6L & 12 & -6L \\ 6L & 2L^2 & -6L & 4L^2 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation}\]

The rows and columns are, in order, the vertical displacement at the start node, the rotation at the start node, the vertical displacement at the end node, and the rotation at the end node.

Step 3: Superpose to Recover the Intended Structure

The stiffness method has solved only the loads on nodes structure. Adding the loads off nodes structure back gives the response of the intended structure.

Displacements at the Nodes

Every node of the loads off nodes structure is locked, so that structure adds nothing to the nodal displacements.

Member End Forces

End forces add directly: the loads on nodes end forces \(\mathbf{k}_e\mathbf{d}_e\) plus the fixed-end forces. The selected member is shown first, followed by both members.

Nodal Forces and Support Reactions

Adding the two structures at each degree of freedom returns the intended loads at free degrees of freedom. At restrained degrees of freedom, the sum is the net force at the support, and removing any load applied directly at that degree of freedom leaves the support reaction.

Shear, Moment, and Deflection

Each row below adds the loads off nodes structure to the loads on nodes structure. The free body diagrams show each member cut away from its nodes. Black arrows are loads between nodes, and orange arrows are the end forces acting on the member. Arrows point in the actual direction of each force, and labels give magnitudes. A load applied directly at a node acts on the node rather than on a member, so it appears in these diagrams only through the end forces. Move across any plot to read the sum at that location.

Positive shear is a net upward force to the left of a cut. Positive moment bends the beam concave up (sagging). In terms of the end forces of a member: \(V(0)=V_S\), \(M(0)=-M_S\), \(V(L)=-V_E\), and \(M(L)=M_E\).
